Edge & Expectancy
A high win rate is not an edge. Enter how a strategy actually behaves and this returns what it earns per trade — plus how many trades you need before the number means anything at all.
R = one unit of risk. If you risk 20 USDT and win 30, that's a 1.5R win. Expectancy is what one trade is worth on average — the only number that decides whether a strategy makes money. Trades needed is how large a sample must be before the result is distinguishable from luck at 95% confidence; below it, a good run is a hypothesis, not a verdict.
🎲
Risk of Ruin
Even a profitable strategy can bury you if you bet too big. Simulates 5,000 runs of your rules to see how often the account is wrecked before the edge shows up.
Risk compounds off the current balance, so losses shrink the bet and full zero is rare — "ruin" is the drawdown you'd never come back from psychologically or mathematically. Re-run for a fresh sample; the number moves a little because it's a simulation, not a formula.

Average Down / Up
Blended entry across multiple fills, and what the position needs to break even. Add a stop to see the real risk from the averaged entry.
Averaging down lowers your break-even but raises your total risk — the stop is now further from a bigger position. This shows both sides so the decision is made with the loss in view, not just the prettier entry price.

Drawdown Recovery
Losses and gains are not symmetric. How far you have to climb back from a hole — the reason capital preservation beats big wins.
